# Reaction-stage mechanism reference

The target is the temperature-dependent MS response during hydrogenation of the already reduced material. Earlier Cu reduction is an activation-stage observation and is outside the scored question.

1. Reaction-state XANES distances from the reduced AuCu reference remain below 0.05 of the precursor-to-reduced distance at both edges. This directly establishes proximity to the reduced reference at the measured stages. The source study independently reports predominantly metallic reaction states and small Cu electronic changes (Figure 5).
2. The Au-edge post-edge comparison now covers 100, 130 and 150 °C. The frozen reference is regenerated from the archive using the existing smoothed-background fingerprint. A separate fifth-degree polynomial background on the same normalized spectra gives within-AuCu correlations of 0.9883, 0.9825 and 0.9772, versus 0.5900 for the reduced Au-only control over 3.5–9.7 inverse angstroms. Across 4–9 and 4.5–9.5 inverse angstroms, the corresponding within-alloy correlations remain above 0.97 and the between-sample correlation below 0.66. This supports persistence of a distinct local environment. Published reduced-state Au–Cu scattering paths (Table 1) supply independent alloy assignment; reaction FT-EXAFS (Figure 6 and Supplementary Figure S8) supplies structural context. Correlation alone does not give a phase fraction, prove fixed coordination, or establish absence of a minority phase.
3. The m/z 54 channel decreases and 56 increases between 130 and 150 °C within AuCu, Au, and AuCu3. The Au control demonstrates that the sign of the temperature response can arise without alloy rearrangement in that control. This is a within-run comparison, not a conversion, selectivity, rate-constant or cross-sample activity measurement. It does not demonstrate the same elementary mechanism in the three samples.

The supported explanation is a temperature-dependent response on a persistently reduced AuCu ensemble with an alloy-like local environment. The reduced ensemble and the same-direction Au control response are scored as separate supported process observations. Large bulk redox or bulk demixing as the dominant explanation lacks its expected ensemble-level signature here. Changes in minority Cu-site populations or in coverage and kinetics on fixed sites are plausible but the data do not identify either as the dominant cause. In particular, persistence of the ensemble structure is not evidence that the active-site population is fixed.

The first two candidate mechanisms are not supported as dominant explanations, while the two minority-site and fixed-site alternatives are unresolved. The evidence does not establish fixed active sites, calibrated yield or selectivity, or a shared elementary mechanism between Au and AuCu.
