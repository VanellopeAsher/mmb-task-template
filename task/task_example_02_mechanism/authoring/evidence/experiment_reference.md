# Reference for discriminating experiments

The archived ramps show earlier apparent Cu reduction in bimetallic samples, and the reaction scans show small bulk XAS changes despite changing mass signals. The archive contains no matched dose intervention or direct measurement of a changing surface Cu site population. These observations motivate the following conditional tests without supplying a numerical effect size. See [the primary operando study](https://doi.org/10.1002/cctc.202500379) and [the data archive](https://doi.org/10.57745/EQL6HT).

- **Exposure effect during activation.** Use fresh matched aliquots, common readout exposure, and a dose series or beam-on/off intervention. Match measured bed temperature history, hydrogen feed, catalyst preparation, and geometry. Follow the Cu reduction midpoint with Cu-edge XAS, or compare the Cu reduction extent at the same time with Cu-edge XAS or validated quench/speciation. An exposure-driven reduction component large enough to detect predicts a dose response. Spillover-only and dispersion/precursor-only explanations predict no systematic exposure response with those controls. A positive result identifies an exposure contribution and allows chemical effects to coexist. A null bounds the exposure contribution over the tested range and leaves thermal bias and chemical alternatives open.
- **Gas-responsive surface redistribution.** Cycle or switch reactant partial pressure with matched bed temperature history, hydrogen feed, total flow and pressure, preparation, and probe exposure. Track the reversible surface Cu population with calibrated operando surface XPS or a validated surface-site probe. Monitor calibrated product rate in parallel to establish whether the perturbation actually changes catalytic response. A gas-responsive redistribution above the detection limit predicts reversible population changes. A stable-site population can support changing rate without a systematic site population response. The assay must resolve population from adsorbate attenuation, chemical shifts, or probe-induced changes. A population change alone does not establish rate mediation. A null bounds redistribution accessible to this perturbation and probe.

In both tests the detection threshold must come from matched repeats and assay calibration. Automatic scoring evaluates the structured causal design within these two contrasts. It does not validate a particular instrument setup or exclude other scientifically viable experiment families.
