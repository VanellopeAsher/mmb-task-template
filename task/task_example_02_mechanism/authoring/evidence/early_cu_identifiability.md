# Cu activation causal identifiability

> Historical activation analysis. Early Cu reduction causality is outside the current reaction-stage reward.

The operational Cu midpoints are 88.29 °C for Cu, 69.88 °C for AuCu, and 74.49 °C for AuCu3 on the resistor scale. All 302 raw Cu-edge ramp files across those three samples report a missing sample-temperature reading. The shift therefore survives a common additive calibration correction, but actual bed temperatures are not independently matched. The reaction MS channels refer to subsequent hydrogenation and do not identify the molecular hydrogen transfer pathway, local water activity, or sample-specific photon dose during activation.

| Candidate | Evidence and verdict |
|---|---|
| Hydrogen spillover | Au can activate hydrogen and the source paper proposes transfer to Cu, potentially via TiO2. Spatially resolved work on Pt/iron oxide establishes a TiO2-mediated precedent, not proof for Au/Cu. The nearly coincident Au and Cu ensemble transitions do not exclude a small early population of reduced Au sites. Compatible. |
| Cu precursor dispersion | The source paper proposes smaller or more dispersed Cu precursor domains as a kinetic explanation. The archive does not independently match precursor size at fixed chemistry. Compatible. |
| Cu–O bonding | The source paper separately proposes weakened Cu–O bonding near Au. This concerns local precursor coordination rather than domain size. Final alloy EXAFS cannot isolate a precursor bond effect. Compatible. |
| Metal nucleation kinetics | In situ CuO reduction studies show nucleation and autocatalysis. Au-rich nuclei could facilitate formation of the reduced phase, even without increasing mobile H supply. Post-reduction alloy structure does not determine the nucleation chronology. Compatible as a hypothesis. |
| X-ray photoreduction | A beam-dependent chemical change in reducibility is distinct from bed thermometry. Published dose and beam-off tests demonstrate the possibility for other supported oxides. The non-XAS TPR trend in related catalysts argues against generalizing a beam-only story across experiments, but the present ramps have no matched dose control for an effect on the observed contrast. Compatible. |
| Sample-dependent bed temperature | Sample-specific thermal lag or offsets could alter the relationship between resistor temperature and the reacting bed. Raw headers lack the sample temperature. Compatible. |
| Local H2/H2O transport | Hydrogen supply and product water affect oxide reduction, including inhibition by product water in a published CuO system. Equal nominal inlet composition does not establish equal local activities across the beds. No local gas or matched transport intervention identifies this contribution. Compatible as a hypothesis. |
| Common temperature offset alone | Adding the same constant to every sample temperature cancels in both contrasts (18.41 and 13.80 °C). Excluded as the sole source of the relative shift. |
| Butadiene required for early reduction | The Cu shift occurs during the H2 activation ramp before the butadiene feed. Excluded as a necessary cause of the observed shift. |

Sources: [original study](https://doi.org/10.1002/cctc.202500379), [raw archive](https://doi.org/10.57745/EQL6HT), [spillover precedent](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature20782), [CuO nucleation and reduction](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022110200942), [beam response](https://doi.org/10.1039/D2CP02721F), [water inhibition](https://doi.org/10.1039/B306132A). The seven retained causes can coexist. Compatibility establishes no ranking or causal attribution.
