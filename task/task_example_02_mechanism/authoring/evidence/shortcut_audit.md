# Shortcut audit

The public article contains fitted coordination numbers and a favored H-spillover interpretation, but the task's raw-data checkpoints use independently recomputed sample-specific XANES midpoints, reaction contrasts, mass-channel windows, and a post-edge fingerprint. A citation-only or paper-label answer fails these checks. The mechanism answer is deliberately narrower than the paper's proposed causal story: earlier Cu reduction is an association, and H spillover remains one of several compatible causes.

An answer that computes all numerical fields but omits `mechanism_comparison` or `discriminating_experiment` receives zero. The verifier rejects a large-bulk-redox requirement, a claim that AuCu-specific restructuring is necessary for the common m/z 54/56 sign, unsupported unique active-site or morphology claims, and experiments without a matched control or an independent discriminating readout. Two distinct controlled-test designs are accepted.

The Agent-facing prompt no longer supplies the mechanism-by-observation prediction rows. The two compatible rows contain `null` where an observation is possible but not necessary, so copying observed signs into every surviving model is rejected. In particular, the measured high AuCu post-edge correlation is not a necessary prediction of population-stable sites: adsorbate or electronic changes could alter that fingerprint without changing the active-site count. Numerical field conventions remain explicit for comparable evidence; they are not a complete mechanistic workflow. The constrained experiment vocabulary limits automatic scoring to two operational rival pairs, and proposed outcomes remain unmeasured. Broader scientifically valid experimental proposals require expert review.

The source dataset itself includes prepared normalized spectra and annotations; those are useful observations, not final mechanism labels. A full Agent trial must still check whether the prompt's structured fields over-constrain the scientific choices and whether a different valid mechanism comparison or experimental design would be unfairly rejected. Five real domain experts have not yet attempted the task under the same environment.
