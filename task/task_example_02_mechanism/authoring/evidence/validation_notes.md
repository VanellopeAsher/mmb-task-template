# Validation notes

The complete XAS archive matched the Dataverse MD5 `085ecb1b86089a542f909c5d867d50b0` and passed ZIP CRC validation. The Oracle computes six activation midpoints and six dimensionless AuCu XANES reaction-change ratios from normalized spectra and raw scan headers. The latter quantify spectral change relative to activation. Published EXAFS coordination numbers and MCR component counts are no longer scored.

The 7,950,132-byte MassSpectrometer.zip archive was downloaded, matched the Dataverse MD5 `6ea7054361b23d8bb6022dd3d0761b86`, and passed ZIP extraction. `inspect_mass_spectrometer.py` independently parses all eight exports: each has 80 mass channels, with scan counts ranging from 2 to 12,475 and median sampling interval about 5.65 s. One AuCu3 file contains only two scans; the Cu run has no in-file annotations. The script checks timestamps and contiguous scan numbers.

For the AuCu run, three 20-minute windows within the annotated 100, 130, and 150 °C reaction stages contain 213, 212, and 212 scans. The 130 °C window is 19:50–20:10, ending before the transition inferred from the annotation "12 min at 150 °C" at 20:28:45. The earlier 20:00–20:20 window was rejected because its final minutes may already be at 150 °C. The corrected raw median m/z 54 signals are 15.232, 12.7795, and 10.5035; m/z 56 signals are 1.5315, 3.7460, and 5.68105. This independently confirms opposing channel trends with temperature in this run. The m/z 42 medians are 1768.5, 1774.9, and 1776.75; m/z 44 medians are 3.4769, 3.5597, and 3.6218; m/z 58 medians are 0, 0, and 0.062088. These are instrument signals, not calibrated species concentrations or a quantitative selectivity estimate. Fragment deconvolution and run controls are still needed before treating molecular selectivity as an independent scored truth.

The source paper reports time-resolved XANES component counts and interprets the Cu transition as Au-assisted reduction. The task scores only the observed earlier Cu midpoint in the bimetallic samples and does not treat that contrast as an identified causal effect of Au. It asks whether the combined site-state and channel evidence favors a large reaction redox change, while preserving the limits on structure, molecular selectivity and rate causality.

The raw-data witness, `solution/witness/reduction_transition.py`, projects each ramp XANES spectrum onto the line joining that sample's initial He and post-reduction H₂ spectra. It interpolates normalized Au and Cu spectra on the respective edge's −10 to +30 eV grid, matches raw scan headers for **resistor** temperature, fits a monotone transition with isotonic regression, and interpolates the 50% crossing. The resulting operational T50 values in °C are Au/Au 54.95 (69 ramp scans), Cu/Cu 88.29 (79), AuCu/Au 68.09 (15), AuCu/Cu 69.88 (16), AuCu₃/Au 70.43 (28), and AuCu₃/Cu 74.49 (28). Each fresh and reduced endpoint is one already averaged export, so scan-level endpoint uncertainty is unavailable. Energy windows of −5…+20, −15…+35, and −10…+50 eV shift any reported T50 by at most 0.72 °C. These values are not the paper's MCR-ALS onset estimates or a direct thermometer reading of the catalyst bed. Their robust qualitative implications are that both bimetallic Cu transitions precede monometallic Cu and that Au and Cu transitions in each bimetallic run are close under this projection. Endpoint choice, temperature metadata, and data-quality exclusions remain sources of uncertainty. The task defines this operational midpoint and grants a 2 °C tolerance.

The earlier evidence-only pilot was regenerated from the two verified source archives and their extracted directory form, with exact agreement. This removed the former paper-answer shortcut, but did not establish a mechanism-discrimination task. A unique AuCu morphology, molecular selectivity, causal rate law, and empirical human difficulty remain unverified.

The mechanism-discrimination revision adds independently annotated 130/150 °C controls. Au/TiO₂ has m/z 54 medians 3.2907→2.5870 and m/z 56 11.983→12.521; AuCu₃/TiO₂ has 13.721→10.990 and 2.5042→4.6566. AuCu has 12.7795→10.5035 and 3.746→5.68105. All three runs show the same sign, so AuCu-specific restructuring is not required for that sign. The exports are uncalibrated; these cross-run levels cannot be compared as rates. Au/Au and Cu/Cu 130/150 °C XANES change ratios are 0.03254/0.03577 and 0.04119/0.04000, respectively; AuCu/Au and AuCu/Cu remain below 0.05. A mechanism requiring bulk redox of at least one tenth of the activation contrast conflicts with all these controls, but minority surface reorganization remains unresolved.

The Au-edge post-edge fingerprint calculation from the released normalized spectra gives Pearson r=0.57852 between reduced Au and reduced AuCu, versus r=0.98245 between reduced AuCu and its 130 °C reaction state. With k ranges 4–9 or 3–10 Å⁻¹ and smoothing windows 81–121 points, the first remains 0.58–0.64 and the second 0.98–0.99. The filter suppresses a smooth background and is not an EXAFS coordination fit; differences can reflect size, disorder, electronic state, alignment and alloying. It supports a distinct reduced local/electronic fingerprint and reaction-stage similarity without identifying an atomic morphology.

The revised reference answer now makes an explicit set-valued causal conclusion: broad bulk state stability and a common temperature response are observable; stable sites and minority surface redistribution both remain compatible, as do multiple explanations for the earlier Cu reduction. It scores one controlled experiment that gives divergent predictions under a chosen rival pair. The two accepted designs are limited to the instrument capabilities named in the task. This is a stronger scientific target than the prior numeric-only pilot, but Agent trials and expert review must test whether the constrained structured experiment output rejects other valid research routes.

The current mechanism comparison is implemented as a three-observation Boolean signature and a four-model necessary-prediction matrix. A model is excluded only when a non-null necessary prediction conflicts with a measured signature; `null` explicitly preserves a mechanism's lack of prediction. Both source ZIPs and their independently extracted directory form regenerate the complete revised answer exactly. The adversarial verifier accepts an alternate controlled test and rejects numeric-only submissions, reversed observations, false necessary predictions, single-mechanism overclaims, missing controls, and non-finite values under ordinary Python and `python -O`.
