# Outcome-based verification requirement for Example 03

The acceptance target is an objective test of whether an experimental plan **actually separates the plausible mechanisms using measured outcomes**. Checking for the names of measurements, a full-rank hypothetical signature matrix, or an exact match to one reference protocol does not establish this. The scientific outcome need not be binary: a plan can exclude some alternatives while leaving others unresolved. Reward should reflect the measured degree of separation, with uncertainty, instead of forcing every design into a 0/1 judgment.

For a prospective design task, the evaluator must have a frozen set of admissible interventions and measurements, observed results for those interventions from a **matched material/device system**, and an independently supported mechanism label or active-mechanism set. The evaluator must keep the actual results hidden from the Agent. The task can then be scored as follows:

1. The Agent chooses a feasible subset of interventions and readouts and reports which competing explanations its resulting observations support or exclude. Its plan determines which hidden experimental outcomes enter the assessment.
2. The verifier recomputes measured effects for the selected readouts, with uncertainty estimated at the independent device/batch level. It compares the observed effects with pre-registered, quantitative predictions for every candidate mechanism. An unmeasured or confounded comparison contributes no exclusion evidence.
3. Reward reflects empirical separation, predictive accuracy on held-out outcomes, and consistency of the reported mechanism with independent evidence, under a declared experimental budget. A quantitative separation score can use the independently calibrated likelihood of the selected outcomes under each competing mechanism, including replicate-level measurement error. The score should give partial credit when observations rule out some mechanisms and full credit only when the surviving alternatives are resolved within a predeclared uncertainty bound. A lucky mechanism label without discriminating evidence cannot earn full reward. The likelihood model, tolerances, weights, budget and uncertainty rule must be frozen before Agent evaluation.

The present Prete et al. data do **not** furnish this ground truth. The authors propose interfacial ionic trapping, while also discussing oxide oxygen effects. Their ToF-SIMS specimens were separately prepared and reached different aging endpoints from the encapsulated T60 recovery devices. Table 1 contains drift-diffusion fitted parameters, not directly measured causal labels. A new matched outcome panel with independent intervention validation, or a different published system with such data, is needed before this scoring rule can be implemented honestly. The existing checklist verifier remains a draft and must not be promoted to the benchmark reward pool.

An available outcome panel may allow a stronger task without inventing results: [Parion et al., Zenodo dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/18423433) contains 1,039 source files across thermal-only ISOS-D2 and light-plus-heat ISOS-L2 stresses, including I–V, photoluminescence, capacitance–frequency and XRD/EQE measurements. The 5,647,351-byte data archive passed its published MD5 (`0985215dfb04dbba281eb6bb46bf0a8a`). The [source paper](https://imec-publications.be/server/api/core/bitstreams/a529ecb1-87c5-4852-bda9-693baa0a3ba8/content) attributes D2 degradation mainly to an interface charge-extraction barrier and L2 degradation to absorber change. A held-out assay-selection experiment can score whether selected measurements actually distinguish those observed regimes. The measurement files, matching, replicate structure and uncertainty require further audit before freezing the oracle.

`recompute_stress_signals.py` now checks two source-level diagnostic outcomes directly from the ZIP. At 0 V and 10–100 kHz, the median capacitance ratio after/before stress is 0.996 for D2 (different devices at the two times), versus 0.459 and 0.510 for the two paired L2 devices. In the two PL spectra available at the initial state, the index-matched peak shifts are -1 and -3 nm for D2 and -21 and -12 nm for L2. The PL filename indices are not yet independently confirmed as identical spatial positions; these are exploratory measurements, not final scoring tolerances. The original PL export also contains repeated nonphysical odd-index traces, so future extraction must freeze a documented quality-control rule.
